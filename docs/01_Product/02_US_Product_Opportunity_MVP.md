# US Product Opportunity MVP

- Version: v0.1
- Status: Draft
- Updated: 2026-08-23

## 1. Goal

Help a product seller discover products and categories that appear to be selling well or gaining momentum in the United States, then understand the customer demand and competitive conditions behind that signal.

The MVP identifies evidence-based opportunities. It must label results as observed sales data, marketplace signals, or inferred demand depending on the source available.

## 2. Primary user

A seller, founder, or product marketer considering a product to sell in the US market who wants to answer questions such as:

- Which categories or products are currently strong or growing?
- Who appears to buy them, and what do they value?
- Why are they selling well?
- What customer pain points and market gaps remain?
- How crowded is the competitive landscape?

## 3. MVP workflow

1. User selects a US category or enters a product idea.
2. User selects the time period and customer segment, if known.
3. The system gathers approved public demand, marketplace, search, review, and competitive signals.
4. The system ranks opportunities by the available evidence and explains the ranking.
5. The user opens a product or category brief and saves opportunities for follow-up.

## 4. Required output

- Products or categories with observed momentum.
- Signal type and source quality for each result.
- Target customer profile and stated preferences.
- Common purchase motivations, pain points, and review themes.
- Competitive landscape and differentiation opportunities.
- Risks, uncertainties, and recommended next research steps.

## 5. First-release boundaries

- Focus on the United States only.
- Begin with a small number of categories and approved sources.
- Do not claim a product is "best-selling" unless a source directly supports that claim.
- Do not estimate revenue, market size, or sales volume without a defined data source and method.
- Treat marketplace and search signals as indicators, not proof of total market sales.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- A user can research a US category or product idea.
- Each ranked result shows its signal type, market, period, and supporting evidence.
- The product brief separates observed facts from AI interpretation.
- The user can save an opportunity and its follow-up questions.
- The system clearly identifies missing or weak evidence.

## 7. Open decisions

- Which categories should be included first?
- Which marketplaces and trend sources can be used under their terms?
- Does the first version need actual sales data, or is it a demand-signal discovery tool?
- What minimum evidence should qualify a product for the opportunity list?
