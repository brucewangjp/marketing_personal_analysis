# US Product Opportunity MVP

- Version: v0.1
- Status: Draft
- Updated: 2026-08-23

## 1. Goal

Help the project owner make US product-development and product-selling decisions through two complementary views:

1. Identify products or categories that are currently selling well, where reliable sales or marketplace evidence is available.
2. Identify emerging unmet needs, conversation themes, and product-creation opportunities before a category becomes crowded.

The MVP identifies evidence-based opportunities. It must label results as observed sales data, marketplace signals, public-discussion signals, or inferred opportunity depending on the source available.

## 2. Primary user

The project owner, considering a product to develop or sell in the US market, who wants to answer questions such as:

- Which categories or products are currently strong or growing?
- Who appears to buy them, and what do they value?
- Why are they selling well?
- What customer pain points and market gaps remain?
- How crowded is the competitive landscape?
- Is this already a heavily copied, saturated opportunity or an earlier-stage opportunity?

## 3. MVP workflow

1. User selects a US category or enters a product idea.
2. User selects the time period and customer segment, if known.
3. The system gathers approved sales, marketplace, search, review, public-discussion, and competitive signals.
4. The system separates current sales leaders from emerging opportunity themes.
5. The system assesses evidence of demand, competition, saturation, and uncertainty.
6. The user opens a product or category brief and saves opportunities for follow-up.

## 4. Required output

- **Current sales reality:** products or categories with observed sales or marketplace momentum.
- **Emerging opportunity signals:** unmet needs, pain points, growing topics, and new product ideas visible in approved online signals.
- Signal type and source quality for each result.
- Target customer profile and stated preferences.
- Common purchase motivations, pain points, and review themes.
- Competitive landscape and differentiation opportunities.
- Risks, uncertainties, and recommended next research steps.
- A clear distinction between “selling well now,” “high attention,” and “promising but unvalidated opportunity.”

## 5. First-release boundaries

- Focus on the United States only.
- Begin with a small number of categories and approved sources.
- Do not claim a product is "best-selling" unless a source directly supports that claim.
- Do not estimate revenue, market size, or sales volume without a defined data source and method.
- Treat marketplace and search signals as indicators, not proof of total market sales.
- Do not treat a popular discussion topic as proof of a viable product opportunity.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- A user can research a US category or product idea.
- Each ranked result shows its signal type, market, period, and supporting evidence.
- The product brief separates observed facts from AI interpretation.
- The user can save an opportunity and its follow-up questions.
- The system clearly identifies missing or weak evidence.
- The system visibly separates mature, crowded opportunities from emerging, less-validated opportunities.

## 7. Open decisions

- Which categories should be included first?
- Which marketplaces and trend sources can be used under their terms?
- Does the first version need actual sales data, or is it a demand-signal discovery tool?
- What minimum evidence should qualify a product for the opportunity list?

## 8. Two-view decision model

| View | Main question | Evidence needed | Key risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current sales reality | What is selling well now? | Licensed sales data or clearly labeled marketplace evidence. | Entering a crowded category too late. |
| Emerging opportunity | What new need or idea is gaining attention but may not yet be crowded? | Search, review, and approved public-discussion signals. | Mistaking discussion or novelty for real demand. |

The product opportunity report should compare both views before recommending what the user should investigate next.
